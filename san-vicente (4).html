The Insight

A green pocket of Camarines Norte all its own.

San Vicente is the only fully landlocked town in a province otherwise framed by the Pacific. Instead of beaches, its pride is the land itself: rolling hills, a narrow river valley, and a south-western shoulder that rises toward the forested slopes of Mount Labo.

It is the least populous of all the towns in Camarines Norte, and that smallness shapes its character — quiet, rural, and tightly woven together. Most of its land is farmland, and the labor of its people reaches tables across the province as rice, coconut, corn, vegetables, and freshwater fish.

The town traces its founding to 1803 — in 2025 it marked its 222nd Founding Anniversary. Before becoming a town in its own right it was a barrio under neighboring Talisay, and it carries the name of its patron, Saint Vincent Ferrer. Today it sits in the second congressional district, roughly nine kilometers inland from the provincial capital, Daet.

The Patron Saint

Who was Saint Vincent Ferrer?

The town's namesake was a Spanish friar and one of the most famous preachers of the late Middle Ages. Born in Valencia around 1350, he joined the Dominican Order — the Order of Preachers — as a teenager, and his brilliance as a student soon made him a professor of theology.

He lived through the Western Schism, a turbulent stretch when rival claimants each declared themselves the true pope, splitting the loyalties of Christian Europe. For a time he served the Avignon claimant as confessor, but the division weighed on him heavily. He eventually left the papal court to do what he is most remembered for: traveling on foot across Spain, France, Italy and beyond, preaching repentance for some twenty years.

His sermons on the coming judgment were so powerful that he became known as the "Angel of the Judgment," and hundreds of miracles were later attributed to him. He died in Vannes, France, in 1419 and was declared a saint in 1455. Fittingly for a town of farmers and fisherfolk, he is honored as a patron of builders, construction workers and fishermen — and, because he labored to heal a divided Church, of reconciliation.

The Pride of San Vicente

What the town is known for.

Mananap Falls

The town's crown jewel — a roughly 60-foot waterfall tucked into the forest, with a clear pool for swimming and a calm spot for fishing. It is reached by a scenic trek of about two kilometers from Barangay Fabrica, and it lends its name to the town's own festival.

Nature · Adventure

St. Vincent Ferrer Parish Church

The spiritual heart of the Poblacion and home to the town's patron. The parish anchors community life and is the focus of San Vicente's biggest yearly celebration each April.

Getting Here & Around

How to reach San Vicente.

Landlocked but far from isolated — the town connects by road to Daet and its neighbors, with several entry points in and out.

🚌

From Manila

Roughly 350 km south. Take a Bicol-bound bus to Daet (about 8–9 hours), the provincial capital and main gateway.

🚐

From Daet

Only about 9 km inland. Hop on a van or jeep along the Daet–San Vicente Provincial Road for the short ride into town.

🛺

Around Town

Tricycles and jeeps are the everyday rides between barangays, with roads linking out to Labo, Vinzons, Talisay, and San Lorenzo Ruiz.

A Day in San Vicente

Life out in the open.

Days here move with the land. Much of life happens outdoors — in the fields, by the river, and along the town's busy little market.

🌾

In the Fields

Farmers tend rice and corn across the lowlands, while coconut and root crops fill the rolling hills — the backbone of the town.

🎣

By the River

With no coast of its own, San Vicente turns to its rivers and streams for freshwater fish and a cool break from the day's heat.

🧺

At the Market

Trading, handicraft, and home-made food keep the Poblacion humming, where neighbors meet and the day's harvest changes hands.

Green & Growing

An eco-friendly town at heart.

San Vicente's pride is its nature — the falls, the rivers, and the forested slopes of Mount Labo. Keeping them clean and green is everyone's task, residents and visitors alike.

💧

Protect the Waters

Keep Mananap Falls and the rivers litter-free so they stay safe for swimming and for the fish the town depends on.

🌳

Guard the Forests

The greenery around Mount Labo shelters wildlife and water sources — worth growing and never burning.

🥾

Travel Lightly

On the trail to the falls, take only photos and leave only footprints — pack out everything you bring in.

♻️

Sort & Reuse

Simple habits — segregating waste and saying no to single-use plastic — keep the barangays clean and the harvest healthy.
